About this tool

Letters are hard to carve freehand because their inside shapes, the holes in A, B, D, O, P, Q and R, fall out when you cut around them. Stencil letters solve this with bridges: small uncut strips that hold each centre in place. This generator sets your text in a stencil typeface, so every letter with a hole comes with its bridges drawn in, ready to print and transfer.

Measure the flattest face of your pumpkin from side to side and enter that width. The generator scales the letters to fit and moves a long word onto two lines or several sheets. Print at 100% rather than 'fit to page', tape the sheet to the pumpkin, then prick along the outlines with a pin or poking tool to transfer the design before you carve.

Letters at least 4 cm (about 1.5 in) tall are easiest with a small carving saw, and the chunky style uses heavier letters with wider bridges, which suits thin-walled pumpkins. Children can choose the word and do the pricking; leave the cutting to an adult. The text you type is drawn in your browser and never sent to us.

Questions

Can I use the stencils for painting instead of carving?

Yes. Cut the letters out of the paper with a craft knife to make a paint stencil, or trace the outlines with a pencil and fill them with paint or a marker.

Why are some letters split into pieces?

That is the bridge: a strip you do not cut, which keeps the middle of letters like O and A attached to the pumpkin.

How do I carve it, step by step?

1. Print at 100% and check the ruler on the sheet (10 cm, or 4 in when you work in inches). 2. Tape the sheet to the flattest side, smoothing it as you go; on a very round pumpkin, cut the sheet between letters and tape each one flat. 3. Prick along every outline. 4. Remove the paper and cut the grey shapes, the small inside pieces first, leaving the bridges uncut. 5. For a long word on several sheets, tape the sheets side by side in order: each sheet is labelled with its sheet number and line.
